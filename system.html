Ship to gate

How a container moves
through the system

Five stages, one continuous record — nothing is re‑entered or re‑checked between them.

01

The operational flow

01

Pre‑advice

Container is registered before it arrives, so gate clerks aren't creating records from scratch at the barrier.

02

Gate In

Plate, transporter and yard block captured once; a position slip is issued automatically.

03

Yard / Quayside / SGR

Held, staged or received off rail — location and clerk logged the moment it happens.

04

Vessel Loading

Loaded against its designated vessel; if it's actually loaded on a different one, a reason is required before the record closes.

05

Departed / Reported

Every movement is already logged — reporting is an export, not a reconstruction.

02

Why it matters

A terminal management system only earns its keep if it removes work, not adds it. Every module below exists because a specific step used to require a radio call, a log book, or someone's memory.

👁️

Full visibility

Dashboard, dwell‑time and vessel‑loading reports show exactly where every container sits, without radio calls to reconstruct it.

🎲

Fewer loading errors

When a container is loaded onto a vessel other than the one it was pre‑advised for, the system requires a documented reason — nothing slips through silently.

⏱️

Faster turnaround

Gate, yard and vessel loading share one record, so nothing is re‑entered or re‑checked at each stage.

🧾

Audit‑ready by default

Every gate‑in, shift, shutout and load is logged with a timestamp and the clerk who recorded it.

🔁

Safe for multiple terminals

Slip, shift, shutout and random‑load numbers are issued centrally, so two clerks working at once never collide.

📡

Cloud‑synced, offline‑resilient

Actions save instantly to your device and sync to the central system in the background, even on a shaky connection.
